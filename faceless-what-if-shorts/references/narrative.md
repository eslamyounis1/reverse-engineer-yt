# Narrative DNA

## Structure: 5 time-stamped chapters

Evidence: 5/5 references have exactly 5 spoken time-stamped chapters. Chapter onsets average
4 / 17 / 37 / 57 / 75% of runtime. The final chapter runs 12–17 s and is the longest.
Confidence: **High**.

| Block | Onset (% runtime) | Length @55 s | Words @3.9 w/s | Job |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0% | 2–2.5 s | 10–14 | The question (see `hooks.md`) |
| Ch 1 "Day one" | ≈ 4% (1.7–3 s) | 6–9 s | 25–35 | Arrival / birth. The world's rules are shown through what happens *to* you. Plant the signature prop or constraint. |
| Ch 2 | ≈ 17% (±4) | 9–12 s | 35–45 | First rite of passage: school, first hunt, training, first night alone. A first small win **or** a first hard cost. |
| Ch 3 | ≈ 37% (±6) | 9–12 s | 35–45 | Competence or status rises. The world widens (new place, bigger scale). A mid-video interrupt. |
| Ch 4 | ≈ 57% (±6) | 9–12 s | 35–45 | **Turn A.** Peak of the premise *or* its inversion (luxury → loneliness; arena → decisive fight). |
| Ch 5 | ≈ 75% (±5) | 12–17 s | 45–65 | **Turn B + payoff.** Highest stakes, the trained skills used, or reality breaks. Reframing line. Callback image. |

**Time-stamp units:**

- **Lifespan premise** (4/5): `Day one → Year a → Year b → Year c → Year d`.
  - Gaps widen or stay even. Observed sets: 10/20/30/40 · 5/15/25/30 · 5/18/35/80 · 3/10/15/20.
  - Choose ages where a real status change happens: school age, adolescence, first job,
    peak career, old age.
- **Bounded premise** (1/5): `Day one … Day five`, or `Hour 1 … Hour 24` split into 5. The stated
  bound in the hook **is** the countdown.
- Always speak the stamp as the **first words** of the chapter's first shot, followed by a colon
  or full stop: "Year ten. You attend…" / "Day four: you hear…".

## Escalation (5/5)

Each chapter must raise stakes on **at least 2** of these axes:

| Axis | Ladder example |
|---|---|
| Physical stakes | cold → hunger → predator → injury |
| Scale of world | room → building → city → planet → universe |
| Social status | owned → trained → feared → free / leader |
| Opponent / threat size | older kids → adults → animals → champion → armed outsiders |
| Psychological | discomfort → fear → hollowness → breaking point → clarity |

## Turn types (choose one for ch 4–5)

| Type | Evidence | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Inversion** | R1 (wealth → emptiness → purpose) | The premise's promise fails; a new value replaces it. |
| **Culmination** | R3 (training → freedom), R5 (training → saves the village) | Everything learned is spent in one decisive event. |
| **Reality break** | R4 (simulation) | The world's rules collapse; end on an open question. |
| **Survival release** | R2 (rescue on the final day) | The bound expires; relief; a warm or comic coda. |

## Payoff and final line

- The final line **reframes the hook**. It answers "what would happen" with meaning, not
  just an event. 4/5 references do this; 1/5 uses a comic/meta closer.
- Keep it ≤ 12 words. The strongest form ends on a single word or a short clause:
  - `You finally found something money can't buy. Purpose.` (pattern: setup + one-word answer)
  - `…they understand you were never the one who needed saving.` (pattern: perspective reversal)
  - `…was just someone's experiment.` (pattern: open dread)
- **No CTA** (5/5). Never say "subscribe", "follow" or "comment".

## Writing rules

1. 2nd person, present tense, all the way through (5/5).
2. One clause per shot, 8–14 words (mean 11). Split longer sentences across 2 shots with no
   ellipsis read aloud; the second image is a carry-over.
3. Prefer concrete nouns, numbers and sensory verbs: "six hours", "30 languages",
   "hands shaking, blade slipping". 5/5 references use specific quantities.
4. Every chapter contains:
   - one **event** (what happens);
   - one **body/sense detail** (cold, cuts, shaking, warmth);
   - one **consequence** or status change.
5. Supporting cast are roles, not names: "the elders", "a private chef", "the instructors",
   "your manager".
6. One comic deflation line per 2 chapters is optional (2/5). It suits spectacle-heavy premises.
   Pattern: amazing thing + mundane complaint ("…but your manager still sends pointless emails").
7. Facts about real eras and places must survive a quick check. Put sources in the manifest.
8. Write numbers as words in the script ("Year ten"). The caption step uses the script's spelling.

## Script template (fill, then count words)

```
HOOK: What would happen if you {condition}?
CH1 Day one. {arrival event}. {sense detail}. {plant: prop/constraint}.
CH2 {Stamp}. {rite of passage}. {cost or win}. {consequence}.
CH3 {Stamp}. {competence/status}. {world widens}. {interrupt beat}.
CH4 {Stamp}. {Turn A: peak or inversion}. {body/psych detail}. {stakes line}.
CH5 {Stamp}. {Turn B: decisive event}. {payoff action}. {REFRAMING LINE}.
```
