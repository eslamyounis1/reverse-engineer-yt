## Script (Sterling voice, speech rate 50, 3.79 words/s measured)

| Chapter | Stamp | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | — | What would happen if you were born under the ocean in 2200? |
| 1 | Day one | Day one. You're born a hundred metres underwater. The air is helium. Your first cry is a squeak. From the surface, your grandparents send a red kite. |
| 2 | Year six | Year six. Red is the first colour the ocean swallows. Outside the window, your kite turns grey. At school, sun lamps keep your bones strong. Even the strictest teacher sounds like a cartoon. |
| 3 | Year fifteen | Year fifteen. You visit the surface for the first time. First, four days sealed in a decompression chamber. The hatch opens. Your voice drops low. The sky has no ceiling. For one windy afternoon, your kite flies red. |
| 4 | Year twenty-two (turn) | Year twenty-two. A hurricane up top rips the power cables loose. The domes go dark. The pumps slow down. Then you remember how hard your kite pulled. You tie it in the deep current outside. It pulls. Hard. |
| 5 | Year thirty (payoff) | Year thirty. Fifty giant kites now fly beneath the city. They never land, never stop. Every light in every dome runs on them. Kids still squeak your name. You didn't need the sky. The ocean had wind all along. |

- **Structure:** lifespan premise, with gaps of 0, 6, 15, 22 and 30 years.
- **Turn:** a culmination. The useless gift (a kite in a city with no wind) becomes the fix for the blackout.
- **Payoff:** a perspective reversal. The ocean had "wind" (current) all along.
- **Plant and payoff:** the red kite. It appears in the hook frame and the gift, goes grey underwater, flies red in the sky, tugs in the current, turns into fifty giant kites, and comes back as the old kite at the window.
- **Comic beats:** the helium squeak, the cartoon teacher, and kids squeaking Pip's name.
- **No CTA.**
- **Different from Mars 2100:** that story's turn was being refused a trip home. This one is about turning a constraint into an invention.
- **Different from the Skill's example:** that plan returns to the surface "home". Here the surface is a one-afternoon visit.
